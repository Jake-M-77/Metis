# Suspect Section

A **suspect section** in an investigation report must be highly structured, objective, and legally sound to ensure clarity and protect the integrity of the case.

## 1. Identifying Information

This section establishes exactly who the person is using objective, verifiable data.

- **Full Legal Name:** Include all given names, middle names, and any known aliases, monikers, or street names.
- **Demographics:** Date of birth, physical description (height, weight, hair/eye color, tattoos, scars), sex, and nationality.
- **Identifiers:** Government ID numbers (driver's license, passport, National Insurance, or Social Security number).
- **Contact Information:** Current and known previous addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses.
- **Employment Details:** Current employer, job title, work address, and professional history if relevant to the case.

## 2. Relationship to the Case

This context explains why this specific individual is a person of interest.

- **Connection to the Incident:** How the suspect is linked to the crime or violation (e.g., employee with access to missing funds, ex-partner of the victim, owner of the vehicle seen at the scene).
- **Connection to Other Parties:** The suspect's relationship to victims, witnesses, or co-conspirators (e.g., supervisor, stranger, acquaintance).
- **Access and Opportunity:** Documentation of whether the suspect had the physical ability, security clearance, or time window to commit the act.

## 3. Evidence and Investigative Findings

This is the core of the suspect section, detailing the facts that connect them to the incident.

### Physical & Digital Evidence

- DNA
- Fingerprints
- Recovered property
- Text messages
- IP addresses
- Computer logs

### Visual & Locational Evidence

- CCTV footage
- Photo lineups
- Dashcam footage
- GPS tracking
- Cell tower data

### Financial Evidence

Where applicable:

- Unusual bank transfers
- Sudden unexplained wealth
- Unauthorized corporate card charges
- Other relevant financial activity

## 4. Statements and Alibis

Document everything the suspect has said and how it compares with the established facts.

- **Suspect Interview Summary:** Dates, times, and key takeaways from formal interviews or informal statements made by the suspect.
- **Alibi Verification:** The suspect's claimed whereabouts during the incident, followed by the investigator's findings confirming or disproving that alibi.
- **Witness Statements:** A brief summary of what witnesses or informants specifically said regarding the suspect's actions or statements.

## 5. Background and History

This section establishes relevant patterns, capability, or potential intent.

- **Criminal/Disciplinary History:** Past arrests, convictions, or internal company disciplinary actions, focusing on similar behavior where relevant to the investigation.
- **Behavioral Notes:** Documented history of threats, sudden changes in behavior, or expressed motives, while keeping the information strictly factual rather than speculative.

## 6. Status and Next Steps

Conclude the section with the current legal or administrative standing of the suspect.

- **Current Status:** Note if the suspect is currently detained, interviewed and released, suspended (in workplace investigations), or at large.
- **Recommended Actions:** Specify the next investigative steps, such as obtaining a search warrant, conducting a follow-up interview, or proceeding with formal charges or termination.

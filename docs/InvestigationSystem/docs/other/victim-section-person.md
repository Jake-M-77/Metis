# Victim Section

A **victim section** in an investigation report must be highly structured, factual, and objective. It serves as the official record of who was harmed, the extent of the impact, and the evidence tying them to the incident.

## 1. Personal Identity & Profile

This section establishes who the victim is and how to contact them.

- **Full Legal Name:** Include any known aliases or maiden names.
- **Demographics:** Date of birth, age, sex, and pronouns.
- **Contact Information:** Current residential address, phone numbers, and email address.
- **Identifiers:** Government ID numbers (e.g., driver's license, passport) where legally permissible.
- **Employment/Status:** Occupation and employer details, especially relevant if the incident occurred at work or involves financial/white-collar crime.

## 2. Relationship to the Subject/Suspect

Establishing the connection between the parties helps establish motive, access, and context.

- **Nature of Relationship:** Specify if the suspect is a stranger, spouse, family member, coworker, neighbor, or acquaintance.
- **History:** Note any documented history of prior conflict, domestic disputes, or restraining orders.

## 3. Statement and Account of the Incident

The victim's direct narrative of what occurred.

- **Chronological Narrative:** A detailed, objective summary of the victim's account of the event.
- **Date, Time, and Location:** The victim's recollection of when and where the incident took place.
- **Interview Details:** The date, time, location, and method (e.g., audio-recorded, written) of the interview, along with the names of the interviewing investigators.

## 4. Injuries and Medical Documentation

For investigations involving physical harm, this section details the physiological impact.

- **Description of Injuries:** Detailed notes on visible injuries, complaints of pain, or psychological trauma noted at the scene.
- **Medical Treatment:** Details of any first aid administered, ambulance transport, or hospital admissions.
- **Medical Records:** References to attached medical reports, forensic nurse examiner (FNE) documentation, or photographs of injuries.

## 5. Property Damage and Financial Loss

For investigations involving theft, fraud, or property crimes.

- **Itemized Loss:** A list of stolen, damaged, or destroyed property.
- **Financial Value:** Estimated or appraised value of the lost assets, including digital assets or currency.
- **Recovery Status:** Note whether any property has been recovered or remains missing.

## 6. Evidence Linked to the Victim

Physical or digital evidence that directly connects the victim to the crime scene or the suspect.

- **Physical Evidence:** Clothing, DNA swabs, fingerprints, or personal items recovered from the scene.
- **Digital Evidence:** Text messages, emails, social media interactions, or phone logs between the victim and suspect.

## 7. Victim Vulnerability & Special Considerations

Factors that may impact the investigation, legal proceedings, or the victim's ability to participate.

- **Vulnerabilities:** Advanced age, youth (minor status), cognitive impairments, language barriers, or physical disabilities.
- **Support Requirements:** The need for translators, legal guardians, advocates, or specific accommodations during interviews.
